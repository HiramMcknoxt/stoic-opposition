UNBOUGHT

You can win bought, or lose clean. Or you can change the rules.
You are a first-time candidate in a fictional county, and you have made one public pledge: no corporate PAC money, no lobbyist money. This game is what that pledge costs. Your only un-buyable currency is time — 10 tokens a week, and a job and a household that need some of them. Act 1: Run Clean. Act 2: Run It Back, Bought. Act 3: Change The Rules.


Act 2 unlocks after your first Act 1 campaign. Act 3 unlocks when you find the empty room — or finish a campaign.

🚪 Doors

Real conversations. Slow, small, permanent. Raises votes and trust.

🎨 DIY

Spray-paint the signs. Make the shirts. Cheap reach, high trust — heavy on time. You don't need money to get your message out. You need something to say.

💼 The Job

You have one. Skip it too many weeks and the household meter — rent, family patience — ends the campaign from outside politics.

💵 Fundraising

Clean: small dollars from real people, slowly. Bought (Act 2): one click, a flood — and a list of favors owed that comes due after you win.

No real people, parties, donors, or companies appear in this game, and it uses no real-world dollar statistics — its economy is time, trust, and favors, on purpose. It collects no data and makes no external calls. Fictional county; the debrief at the end is real.

The real case

On January 23, 2026, the Democratic Party of Lonoke County adopted §601.4 of its bylaws — 15 votes, quorum met. The bylaw lets the county committee designate donors that are “grossly misaligned in their mission, message, or operations” with the party platform. The committee, its auxiliaries, and its own candidates may not take money from a designated donor. Money that slips through is refunded within 30 days.

The committee’s designated list, per the Arkansas Secretary of State’s spreadsheet (May 17, 2026), names 15 Walmart-family entities.

Nobody donated their way to that result. They showed up, took seats, and stayed for the vote.

The committee room is empty. Show up and take the chairs.

That is the whole method of The Stoic Opposition: geographic audits, quorum and seat-taking in local committees with open seats, then a clean-money floor vote — until, race by race, both candidates are unbought.
