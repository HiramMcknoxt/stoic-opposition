The Committee Room Is Empty.

Good. That’s the opportunity.

Political parties are not identities. They are infrastructure: ballot lines, bylaws, bank accounts, endorsements — and rooms where a handful of people make binding decisions about all of it. A county party committee is the local body of a political party — Republicans and Democrats each have one — where residents can claim open seats and vote on the rules the party runs by. In a lot of counties those rooms are understaffed. Some are effectively vacant. Often, nobody is watching the door.

The room is the whole game. Find yours. Take a seat. Change the rule. Show the receipt.

Audit My County See the Receipt

RECEIPT — EXHIBIT A

LONOKE COUNTY, ARKANSAS
Democratic Party of Lonoke County · 15 votes · Quorum met · §601.4 adopted Jan 23, 2026

RESULT: A BINDING BYLAW ON CAMPAIGN MONEY

Source: Democratic Party of Lonoke County bylaws, §601.4 — Ledger entry L-1

Not a pledge. Not a press release. A bylaw, adopted by a county party committee, that binds the committee, its auxiliaries, and its own candidates. It works by designation: the committee names donors grossly misaligned with its platform, and committee money may not come from them. The committee’s designated list, per the Arkansas Secretary of State’s spreadsheet (May 17, 2026), names 15 Walmart-family entities. The same mechanism is available to any county committee, Republican or Democratic. Read the entry in the Living Ledger or see how the mechanism works.

The horizon: both candidates unbought

Imagine a general election where neither candidate took corporate PAC or lobbyist money, and neither owed anything to national party leadership. The debate could finally be about governance, because there would be far less left to sell. That future is not built with a third party, a viral post, or a donation link. It is built county by county, committee by committee, rule by rule — by people willing to sit in folding chairs on a Tuesday night.
