The Committee Room Is Empty.

Good. That’s the opportunity.

Political parties are not identities. They are infrastructure: ballot lines, bylaws, bank accounts, endorsements — and rooms where a handful of people make binding decisions about all of it. In a lot of counties those rooms are understaffed. Some are effectively vacant. Nobody is guarding the door, because nobody thinks the room matters.

The room is the whole game. Find yours. Take a seat. Change the rule. Show the receipt.

Count the chairs and find the governing documents. Do the arithmetic on how few people actually control the room. Change a binding rule instead of passing a sentiment. Then publish the result — and revisit whether it worked. The full method is on the Playbook page.

The horizon: both candidates unbought

Imagine a general election where neither candidate took corporate PAC or lobbyist money, and neither owed anything to national party bosses. The debate would have to be about governance, because there would be nothing else left to sell. That future is not built with a third party, a viral post, or a donation link. It is built county by county, committee by committee, rule by rule — by people willing to sit in folding chairs on a Tuesday night.
