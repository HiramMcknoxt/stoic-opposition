Against the Chameleons

Why You Can't Fake Who You Are

I hear this idea all the time from folks who are completely exhausted with politics:

"Corey, I live in a county that votes 80% Republican. A Democrat can't win dogcatcher here. I'm just gonna register as a Republican, tell folks what they want to hear, and then govern like a progressive once I get elected."[cite: 1]

I see conservatives in places like Chicago and Portland try to pull the exact same trick in reverse.

I get the temptation. I really do. But it's a terrible idea.

First off, people aren't stupid. Your neighbors have an incredible radar for phoniness[cite: 1]. If you're faking your core convictions, they'll know it before you finish your first cup of coffee at the local diner[cite: 1]. The second you get caught trimming your sails, you lose every bit of trust you spent years building[cite: 1].

Second, living like that will eat your soul alive. Pretending to believe things you hate every single day just to keep a seat on a quorum court or a school board will make you miserable[cite: 1]. You'll isolate yourself from the people who actually share your values, and you won't even respect the people you're trying to fool[cite: 1].

The French philosopher Emmanuel Levinas talked about how our primary moral duty begins when we look another human being in the face. You can't look your neighbor in the eye and build genuine solidarity if your whole public life is based on a lie.

The Stoic Opposition isn't about being sneaky[cite: 2]. It's about being sound.

If you believe in labor unions, public investment, and the social safety net, stay in the Democratic Party. Go to your local committee, push out the consultants, kick out the corporate PAC checks, and give working people a party that actually fights for them[cite: 2].

If you believe in small government, fiscal discipline, and local control, stay in the Republican Party. Go into that room, push out the grifters, fight the corporate subsidies, and hold the line on basic integrity.

We don't need everyone to agree on everything. We just need people who have the guts to stand up in their own house and tell the truth.

We're better than playing pretend[cite: 1].
