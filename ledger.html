The Living Ledger

Claims, with receipts.

Dynamic Synthesism keeps a ledger: every mechanism this project points to, with its status and its evidence — including the ones still in process and the ones whose record is honestly unresolved.

Rules of the Ledger. Every entry carries a source, a status, and a date. Statuses are conservative: a thing is ADOPTED only when the record says it was adopted. Retired and failed entries stay visible, with the reason. If an entry here is wrong, the correction goes in the entry — not in a quiet edit.

Exhibit L-1 Adopted

Lonoke County §601.4 — “Campaign Finance Limitations”

Democratic Party of Lonoke County bylaws · Adopted Jan 23, 2026

The proof case. The bylaw works by designation, not by naming categories: the County Committee designates entities “grossly misaligned in their mission, message, or operations” with the party platform. The ban binds the committee itself, its auxiliaries, and its own candidates and nominees — none of them may accept funds from a designated entity. The Secretary maintains the proscribed list, which is publicly available and distributed to candidates; additions and removals are proposed from the floor and voted at the following meeting by simple majority. A contribution that slips through is refunded within 30 days, and the Rules Committee investigates suspected violations and recommends remediation.

Exhibit L-2 Adopted

Cabot Ordinance No. 21 of 2026 — AI religious exemption

City of Cabot, Arkansas · Authored by Corey Spangler · Adopted Aug 17, 2026, unanimous

No city employee may be compelled, as a condition of employment, to use generative or agentic AI in a way that conflicts with sincerely held religious beliefs. The employee files a written First Amendment Technological Accommodation request with HR; the city provides alternative traditional mechanisms; performance and quality standards do not change. Assistive technology — spellcheck, calculators, search — is explicitly not covered. Written into law in advance, instead of handled one employee at a time after the fact.

Exhibit L-3 In Process

Cabot Ordinance No. 33 of 2026 — third-party ALPR funding ban

City of Cabot, Arkansas · Sponsored by Corey Spangler · Second reading passed Nov 2, 2026

The ordinance bars the appropriation or expenditure of city funds for third-party automated license-plate reader systems. It is deliberately generic — the text names no vendor. On November 2, 2026, a motion to waive the third reading and pass it immediately failed 4–4 (a waiver requires two-thirds of all members). The ordinance therefore goes to its third reading at the next regular council meeting, expected November 16, 2026, where an ordinary majority decides. Status will be updated here when the record moves.

Exhibit L-4 Status Unresolved in the Record

State-level “Plebocracy” amendment — Democratic Party of Arkansas

DPA State Committee · 2025

Corey authored the “Plebocracy: Pro-Democracy, Anti-Oligarchy” amendment for the Democratic Party of Arkansas State Committee — the state-level version of the designation design that later became §601.4 in Lonoke County. An April 2025 adoption was reported (85–72), but the sequence is ambiguous in the record available to this project: versions were prepared for more than one meeting, and the reporting does not settle which text was adopted, or whether the reported vote was final. This entry stays labeled unresolved until a document settles it. The county-level mechanism above is the verified one; claims about being “first” are scoped to that specific rule and rest on no counterexample having been found — not on exhaustive proof.
