How §601.4 actually works

Section 601.4, “Campaign Finance Limitations,” was adopted by the Democratic Party of Lonoke County on January 23, 2026. Its design is a designation model:

  1. The committee names the problem, case by case. The County Committee designates specific entities — any organization, corporation, or private individual — as “grossly misaligned in their mission, message, or operations” with the party platform.
  2. The ban binds everyone the committee controls. The committee itself, its auxiliaries, and its own candidates and nominees may not accept funds from a designated entity.
  3. The list is public and maintained. The Secretary keeps the proscribed list; it is publicly available and distributed to candidates.
  4. The list changes in the open. Additions and removals are proposed from the floor and voted at the following meeting by simple majority — so a designation can be challenged in the room, on the record.
  5. Mistakes get unwound. A contribution from a designated entity is refunded within 30 days.
  6. Violations have a process. The Rules Committee investigates suspected violations and recommends remediation.

Why design it this way? A committee cannot amend its platform every time a new donor category appears, and a fixed list of banned categories goes stale or gets lawyered around. A designation model lets the membership itself decide, in public, whose money is incompatible with its stated platform — and makes that decision revisable by the same body that made it.

Adapted Template

Template: Campaign Finance Limitations

An outline implementing the same designation model for another committee. Bracketed fields are yours to fill. This is a starting text for a floor fight, not a finished legal instrument.

ARTICLE [X]: CAMPAIGN FINANCE LIMITATIONS

Section 1. Intent. The intent of this article is to ensure that the [Committee] remains accountable to the citizens of [County], and to no contributor whose mission, message, or operations are grossly misaligned with the [Platform].

Section 2. Designation of proscribed entities. The [Committee] may, by vote, designate any organization, corporation, or private individual as a proscribed entity upon a finding that the entity is grossly misaligned with the [Platform]. A designation is proposed at one regular meeting and voted at the following regular meeting by simple majority.

Section 3. Public list. The Secretary shall maintain the list of proscribed entities, make it publicly available, and distribute it to all candidates and nominees of the [Committee].

Section 4. Prohibition. The [Committee], its auxiliaries, and its candidates and nominees shall not solicit or accept contributions from a proscribed entity.

Section 5. Refunds. Any contribution received from a proscribed entity shall be refunded within thirty (30) days of receipt or of designation, whichever is later.

Section 6. Enforcement. The Rules Committee shall investigate suspected violations of this article and recommend remediation to the [Committee].

Say it on the floor

A spoken script for introducing the template under New Business:

“Under New Business, I move to adopt Article [X], Campaign Finance Limitations, as distributed. The short version: this committee keeps a public list of entities we ourselves judge to be grossly misaligned with our platform, and we — and our candidates — do not take their money. Designations are proposed at one meeting and voted at the next, in the open. If money slips through, it goes back within thirty days. I am not asking anyone to trust a candidate’s willpower. I am asking us to write the rule down so willpower is not required.”

Implementation checklist

  • Confirm your bylaws’ amendment procedure: notice required, vote threshold, when amendments take effect.
  • Print the template and the current bylaws; bring both to the meeting.
  • Count your quorum before you count your votes — run the quorum calculator.
  • Move adoption under New Business; expect the designation votes themselves to happen at later meetings.
  • After adoption: hand the Secretary the list-keeping duty in writing, and calendar the first designation proposals.
  • Publish the adopted text and the list. A private rule is a rumor.
  • Report the outcome to the Living Ledger — including if it fails.

How this differs from a simple direct ban: a direct ban names categories up front (“no corporate PAC money, no lobbyist money”) and freezes them into the text. The §601.4 design bans by designation: the committee judges specific entities against its own platform, in public, one vote at a time, and can revisit the judgment. The direct ban is simpler to say. The designation model is harder to evade and harder to abuse quietly — and it is the one that was actually adopted in Lonoke County.

Legal caveat: this template is not legal advice. Party bylaws interact with state election law and each party’s state rules. Have local counsel or an experienced parliamentarian review any text before adoption.
