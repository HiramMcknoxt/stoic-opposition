The Playbook

The Plebeian Reclamation

A dual-track, hostile-but-lawful takeover of local party committees from within — by the people the parties were built to represent.

The philosophy: Dynamic Synthesism

Governance is an operational problem, not a team sport. Dynamic Synthesism is the working philosophy underneath everything on this site:

  • Efficacy over aesthetic. A policy is judged by whether it works, not by which tribe it flatters.
  • Synthesize, keep, or retire. Ideas from anywhere are tested, combined, and either kept, improved, or retired — with the verdict recorded in the Living Ledger.
  • Sophistry vs. Stewardship. Leaders are graded on one axis: are they performing, or are they passing clean-government rules and doing the work?
  • Parties are infrastructure, never identity. A party is a ballot line, a set of bylaws, and a room. You do not identify with the water department. You show up to the meeting where its rates are set.

The operation, in three phases

Phase 1 — Geographic Audits

Map the terrain county by county: committee rosters, seat counts, vacancy rates, meeting schedules, and the bylaws that govern each room. The audit turns a vague complaint (“politics is broken”) into a number (“this committee has 40 seats, 17 filled, and a quorum of 9”). The Take a Seat page walks you through running one.

Phase 2 — Quorum & Seat Acquisition

Small cadres — three to five serious people — claim vacant precinct and committee seats by filing the form and attending the meetings. In a half-empty committee, a handful of reliable attendees is not a faction. It is a quorum problem for the existing machine, and a governing majority in waiting.

Phase 3 — The Clean-Money Floor Vote

Once seated, move the mechanism: a binding bylaw that cuts the committee, its auxiliaries, and its candidates off from money the committee itself designates as misaligned with its platform. Not a resolution. Not a pledge. A rule, adopted on the floor, with a public list, refunds, and an investigation process. Lonoke County did this on January 23, 2026.

The Four Pillars

  1. Dynamic Applied Synthesism. Treat governance as an operational science: throw out the dead ideological maps and engineer systems that actually work.
  2. The Anti-Politician Scorecard. Stop grading leaders left vs. right. Grade them Sophistry vs. Stewardship: clean-government rules passed, or cameras performed for?
  3. The Plebeian Reclamation. Move serious people into the vacant and poorly defended committee seats and take the machinery back.
  4. The Policy Laboratory. An open-source library of model bills, ordinances, and bylaws — ready to copy, adapt, and introduce at a local meeting. Words shown before power is asked for.

An honest note on tracks: the Reclamation is dual-track. The mechanics — audit, quorum, binding bylaws — work inside either party, in any county, because they operate on procedure rather than ideology. Corey Spangler’s current operational home is the Democratic Party in Lonoke County, Arkansas, where he serves as county chair. That is disclosed, not hidden, because the project asks for the same honesty it practices: work inside the room where you can operate in good faith.
