The Blank Slate: Why We Are Taking Back Our Parties From the Inside Out

I've been thinking a lot about why good people run away from public service[cite: 4].

Whenever I try to recruit folks around here to run for school board or city council (people who run small businesses, trade guys, working moms who know how to manage a budget) they always hit me with the exact same line:

"I'm just not political, Corey. I'm not a politician. That stuff is toxic."[cite: 4]

They're completely right[cite: 4]. What happens in our capitol buildings and on cable news isn't stewardship[cite: 4]. It isn't applied problem-solving[cite: 4]. It's just doing politics[cite: 4]. And doing politics is the whole problem[cite: 4].

I'm in my second term on the Cabot City Council[cite: 1, 4]. I chair the Lonoke County Democratic Committee, and I sit on the State Committee of the Democratic Party of Arkansas[cite: 1, 4]. It still feels weird when someone calls me a politician[cite: 4].

I don't think of myself that way[cite: 4]. I was reading Slavoj Žižek's Against Progress a while back, and it hit me that I'm basically just a grad student who got ahold of a little bit of local authority[cite: 4]. I'm just trying to figure out how to use it so working-class people don't keep getting run over[cite: 4].

A few years ago, I came across the story of the original Stoic Opposition in Rome[cite: 4]. They were a group of philosophers around Emperor Nero who didn't launch a doomed civil war[cite: 4]. Their entire legacy came down to one simple, dangerous stance: they just refused to go along with the bullshit[cite: 4].

For a while, I thought maybe our project should just be pure pragmatism: tell folks to register with whatever party is in power where they live, infiltrate it, and take it over.

I was wrong.

Telling people to be political chameleons is fundamentally dishonest. It asks you to lie about who you are, and your neighbors will smell that from a mile away.

Even if we purge every dime of corporate PAC cash from this country tomorrow, we are still going to have real disagreements about economics, zoning, and how we live together. That's fine. What isn't fine is that both parties have been hollowed out into marketing shells that take money from whoever will give it and leave regular people in the dirt[cite: 4].

We don't need opportunists. We need people who will go into the party that actually speaks to them and do the unglamorous administrative work to fix it from the bottom up[cite: 2, 4].

And here's the thing almost nobody realizes: the machinery is basically undefended.

Local party committees across this country are ghost towns. Precinct seats sit empty year after year.

Most people see an empty room and think it's hopeless. I look at an empty room and see a blank slate.

When a room is empty, you don't have to spend a fortune fighting an entrenched machine. You just have to show up with a few friends.

That's what we did in Lonoke County[cite: 4]. We had a blank slate, so we swung for the fences[cite: 4]. We drafted, introduced, and passed a binding rule that bans corporate PAC money from our county party[cite: 4]. We didn't ask Washington for permission[cite: 4]. We just used the rules that were already on the books[cite: 4].

This project, The Stoic Opposition, is here to help you do the exact same thing in your own county[cite: 4].

Pick the party you actually believe in. Find the empty seats. Walk in, take the chairs, and pass the rule[cite: 4].

We're better than this current mess. Let's get to work.

Strategic Master Plan

How We Take Back Local Party Infrastructure From the Inside Out

Does it not bother y'all that both parties spend billions of dollars every two years telling us the world is ending, while the actual towns we live in keep falling apart?[cite: 1, 4]

They treat politics like a football game. You pick a jersey, you scream at the screen, and you send fifteen bucks to some political consultant in Washington who has never set foot in Arkansas[cite: 4]. Meanwhile, local infrastructure rots, working people get squeezed to death, and corporate PACs write the checks that keep everybody quiet[cite: 4].

I'm done with that[cite: 4].

The Stoic Opposition is a simple, decentralized project[cite: 2, 4]. We aren't starting an underfunded third party that disappears after November[cite: 4]. We aren't telling people to go yell at primary debates[cite: 4]. We are going to find the empty rooms where party decisions are legally made, walk inside, take the seats, and pass rules that kick corporate money out of the building[cite: 4].

Core Operating Principles

1. Go where you actually belong. I don't believe in being a political chameleon. If you lean left, you need to be working inside the Democratic Party. If you lean right, you need to be working inside the Republican Party. Even if we kicked every dime of corporate PAC cash out of politics tomorrow, we'd still have real disagreements about tax rates, zoning, and how big local government ought to be. That's fine. What isn't healthy is lying about what you believe just to win an election. We want honest people cleaning up their own house.

2. The blank slate is our biggest leverage. Here's the secret the establishment doesn't want you to notice: nobody shows up to local party meetings. In a red county, the local Democratic committee is often just four or five retirees in a library basement trying to keep the lights on. In a deep blue city, the local Republican committee is completely dead. People look at that and see failure. I look at it and see a clean workbench. When a room is empty, you don't need a million dollars to win. You just need three to five serious people who show up, learn the rules, and take the open seats.

3. The non-negotiable rule: no corporate PAC cash. We don't police people's personal beliefs, but we do have one iron rule[cite: 4]. If you're organizing with us, you commit to passing a bylaw that bans corporate PAC money and registered lobbyist cash from your local party committee[cite: 4]. Period.

The Three Practical Steps

  • Step 1: The Intake. We use an intake form to map where people live down to the county and precinct level. If three people in the same county sign up, they become an operational cell.
  • Step 2: The Audit and Filing. The cell calls the county clerk or the state party and asks for the official committee roster. They find the empty precinct seats. In most states, getting appointed or filing to run for an empty precinct seat takes five minutes and zero filing fees.
  • Step 3: The Floor Vote. Once seated, members introduce our model bylaw amendment to ban corporate PAC contributions[cite: 4]. We did it in Lonoke County, and it works[cite: 4]. Once the money is out, the grifters leave, and you can start running regular people for quorum court and city council[cite: 4].

You can't want things to stay like this. It's time to show up.

The Lonoke County Blueprint

What Happens When You Show Up to an Empty Room

Have y'all ever actually been to a county committee meeting on a Tuesday night?

Most people think party politics is run like a spy movie, with powerful operatives in smoke-filled rooms deciding everything.

It really isn't. Most of the time it's six people sitting on metal folding chairs in a public library basement, arguing about what kind of sheet cake to buy for an annual dinner.

In Lonoke County, we realized that kind of low attendance isn't a problem[cite: 4]. It's an open door[cite: 4].

If a county committee has forty seats on paper, but only six people show up to the meetings, four people are an absolute majority. That's just basic math. And under almost every state's party rules, if a precinct seat is empty, the folks in the room can vote to fill that vacancy right then and there.

We had an empty room in Lonoke County, so we didn't waste our breath passing meaningless resolutions about national news stories[cite: 4]. We dealt with what was right in front of us: our own committee, our own bank account, and our own integrity[cite: 4].

We brought an amendment to our county bylaws. It did two simple things:

  1. It defined corporate PACs and lobbyists in plain English[cite: 4].
  2. It banned our county committee from taking their money or endorsing candidates who take it[cite: 4].

Because the room was a blank slate, there wasn't a room full of corporate lobbyists sitting there to stop us[cite: 4]. We made the case that working people will never trust a party that takes corporate checks, we called the vote, and it passed[cite: 4].

George Washington warned us in his farewell address about parties becoming tools for unprincipled men to subvert the power of the people. He was dead right. But they only get away with it when normal people stay home.

Stop waiting on somebody from the top of the ticket to come save your county[cite: 4]. They aren't coming. Walk into the empty room and take the gavel yourself.

Against the Chameleons: Why You Can't Fake Who You Are

I hear this idea all the time from folks who are completely exhausted with politics:

"Corey, I live in a county that votes 80% Republican. A Democrat can't win dogcatcher here. I'm just gonna register as a Republican, tell folks what they want to hear, and then govern like a progressive once I get elected."

I see conservatives in places like Chicago and Portland try to pull the exact same trick in reverse.

I get the temptation. I really do. But it's a terrible idea.

First off, people aren't stupid. Your neighbors have an incredible radar for phoniness. If you're faking your core convictions, they'll know it before you finish your first cup of coffee at the local diner. The second you get caught trimming your sails, you lose every bit of trust you spent years building.

Second, living like that will eat your soul alive. Pretending to believe things you hate every single day just to keep a seat on a quorum court or a school board will make you miserable. You'll isolate yourself from the people who actually share your values, and you won't even respect the people you're trying to fool.

The French philosopher Emmanuel Levinas talked about how our primary moral duty begins when we look another human being in the face. You can't look your neighbor in the eye and build genuine solidarity if your whole public life is based on a lie.

The Stoic Opposition isn't about being sneaky[cite: 4]. It's about being sound.

If you believe in labor unions, public investment, and the social safety net, stay in the Democratic Party. Go to your local committee, push out the consultants, kick out the corporate PAC checks, and give working people a party that actually fights for them[cite: 4].

If you believe in small government, fiscal discipline, and local control, stay in the Republican Party. Go into that room, push out the grifters, fight the corporate subsidies, and hold the line on basic integrity.

We don't need everyone to agree on everything. We just need people who have the guts to stand up in their own house and tell the truth.

We're better than playing pretend.

Model Bylaw: Prohibited Corporate Contributions

For Adoption by County Central Committees

"This is the baseline language we used to protect our county committee in Lonoke County from corporate money[cite: 4]. You don't need a high-priced lawyer to pass this. You just need to bring it up under new business, move to adopt, and force a roll-call vote." — Corey[cite: 1]

ARTICLE [X]: PROHIBITED CONTRIBUTIONS AND FINANCIAL INTEGRITY

Section 1. Intent.
The intent of this article is to ensure that the Committee remains accountable strictly to the citizens and working families of [Insert County Name], free from the influence of corporate political action committees and paid lobbyists[cite: 4].

Section 2. Definitions.
(a) "Corporate PAC" means any political action committee established, funded, or controlled by a for-profit corporation, trade association, or corporate entity.
(b) "Registered Lobbyist" means any individual registered to lobby municipal, county, state, or federal government on behalf of paying corporate clients.
(c) "Prohibited Contribution" means any financial donation, transfer, loan, or in-kind gift originating from a Corporate PAC or Registered Lobbyist.

Section 3. Ban on Direct Committee Receipts.
The Committee and its officers shall not solicit, accept, or deposit any Prohibited Contribution into Committee accounts. Any Prohibited Contribution delivered to the Committee shall be returned to the donor within fourteen (14) days[cite: 4].

Section 4. Candidate Endorsements and Committee Resources.
The Committee shall not endorse, financially support, or provide coordinated campaign resources to any candidate for public office who actively solicits or accepts Corporate PAC contributions during the relevant election cycle[cite: 4].

Section 5. Transparency and Inspection.
The Treasurer shall present an itemized balance sheet of all receipts and disbursements at every regularly scheduled meeting. Any member of the Committee in good standing has the right to inspect all banking records upon written notice to the Executive Committee.
