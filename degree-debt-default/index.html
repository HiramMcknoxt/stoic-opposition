DEGREE, DEBT, DEFAULT

You are the president of Alderbrook University (○ a fictional mid-size university) for ten years. Set tuition, amenities, marketing, financial aid, and who teaches. Grow the rankings. Grow enrollment. Keep the reserves healthy.

The money arrives as student loans, and loans arrive whether or not a student ever graduates. Your rankings formula — printed on the president's dashboard, where everyone can see it — counts spending and selectivity. It does not count graduation rates. And if a former student defaults on a loan, the loss is booked to the student and the federal loan program. The university's share of every default, in every year, under every strategy, is $0.

In the corner of your screen, all ten years, is one student. Her name is Maya (○ fictional). Her debt is calculated from your tuition and aid choices. Nothing in her panel feeds any of your numbers. It never will. That is the design — this game's, and the real one's.

About 8 minutes. Five levers a year, ten years, three ways it can end. Every figure is labelled: ◆ sourced · ◇ assumed · ＝ calculated · ○ fictional.
