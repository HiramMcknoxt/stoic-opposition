Explanation of Benefits — what would you do differently?
This is not a bill. It says so right on it — and then the bills arrive anyway, one envelope at a time, from people who were all in the same room. You are the patient. You did everything right: you have insurance, you went in-network, you needed care three times this year. Open the mail. Read every line. Then answer the only question this game asks: what would you do differently?
Nobody in this game is a villain. There is no villain. There is only arithmetic — every figure on every bill reconciles, and you can check it. The plan below is fictional; the margin and waste figures in the sources panel are real and cited.

📚 Sources & real numbers

◆ Typical hospital operating margin ≈ 2%. Kaufman Hall National Hospital Flash Report: about 2.1% for calendar 2024, roughly 1.7–2.5% through 2025. Hospital and facility bills in this game use 2%.

◆ Health insurer profit margins are typically no more than a few percent of premiums — about 2–4% net (KFF, 2026). Thin margins on enormous volume is still enormous money; the game shows margins per bill, and the plan's own admin-and-margin slice is governed by the next figure.

◆ The ACA medical loss ratio: plans must spend 80–85% of premiums on care and quality improvement, leaving 15–20% for administration and profit combined. That is a legal cap on the insurer's slice — note that provider-side administration (hospital billing departments, coding, claims on both ends) sits outside that cap, inside the allowed amounts you just read.

◆ Waste: Shrank et al., JAMA 2019, estimated total waste in US health care at $760–935 billion a year — roughly 25% of spending — with administrative complexity the largest single domain, about $266 billion a year. Broader estimates put administration at 15–25% of all health spending. In this game, administration took the share shown on your end screen — computed from the bills, not asserted.

◇ Disclosed scenario assumptions (labelled ◇ on the bills): non-hospital margin rates (physician, surgery, anesthesia, radiology, laboratory), the provider delivery costs, the billed charges, and the plan terms are fictional-but-typical scenario parameters, chosen so the arithmetic is clean enough to check by hand. The billed-to-allowed gaps are large because real chargemaster gaps are large; any specific bill here is an illustration, not a claim about a real provider.

The formula on every bill (so you can audit us): Allowed = delivery cost + administration + margin. Billed = Allowed + contractual write-off. You owe + plan paid = Allowed. You owe = copay, or deductible-remaining first, then 20% coinsurance on the rest — never more than what is left of your $4,000 out-of-pocket maximum.
