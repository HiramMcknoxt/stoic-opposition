The Argument Lab

Premise Check: the Rhetorical Logic Audit.

We autopsy the argument, not the person. One political line at a time, slowed down and rebuilt where everyone can see the moving parts.

YouTube Instagram TikTok

The method

  1. Reconstruct. Restate the argument as premises and a conclusion — in the strongest form the speaker’s own words support.
  2. Expose the hidden premise. Every punchy line hides one. Find it and put it on the screen.
  3. Name the fallacy — if there is one. If the honest reconstruction is not fallacious, we pick a different clip.
  4. Steelman. State the best version of the other side before grading anything.
  5. Verdict. Scored on the only axis this project uses: Sophistry vs. Stewardship.

The same test applies to everyone. Naming a fallacy does not prove a conclusion false — it proves that argument did not prove it.

Three formats

Format 1

Logic Audit

The full autopsy: premises, validity, hidden premise, fallacy, steelman, verdict and scorecard.

Format 2

Lie Exposure

No logic breakdown. The claim is stated, the verified facts that refute it are laid out with sources, and the episode closes on what the lie was doing.

Format 3

Fallacy Spotlight

One named fallacy, taught plainly — definition, skeleton, and the same move at work in a verified modern example.

The fallacy catalog

The working reference for Fallacy Spotlights: 8 formal fallacies and 24 informal ones, each with a verified modern example in the production file.

Formal — the form itself is broken

Affirming the Consequent · Denying the Antecedent · Affirming a Disjunct · Undistributed Middle · Illicit Process · Four Terms · Masked Man · Existential Fallacy

Informal — relevance

Ad Hominem · Straw Man · Red Herring · Tu Quoque (Whataboutism) · Poisoning the Well · Guilt by Association · Appeal to Authority · Appeal to Popularity · Appeal to Emotion (Fear)

Informal — presumption

False Dilemma · Begging the Question · Appeal to Ignorance · Hasty Generalization · False Cause · Slippery Slope · Cherry Picking · Moving the Goalposts · Weak Analogy · False Compromise

Informal — ambiguity, and the guardrail

Equivocation · Loaded Question · Fallacy of Composition · Fallacy of Division · The Fallacy Fallacy (our guardrail: a bad argument does not prove the conclusion false)

Corrections log

Ep. 002 (Talarico) — published and pulled the same day, Nov 4, 2026. The audit read a figurative line literally: by “money” the speaker meant wealthy interests using wealth to influence an election, and the episode attacked a claim he had not made. Rule adopted: reconstruct with the speaker’s evident meaning; if the honest reading isn’t fallacious, pick a different clip. The episode stays pulled. The rule stays posted.
