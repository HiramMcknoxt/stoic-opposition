Strategic Master Plan

How We Take Back Local Party Infrastructure From the Inside Out

Does it not bother y'all that both parties spend billions of dollars every two years telling us the world is ending, while the actual towns we live in keep falling apart?[cite: 1, 2]

They treat politics like a football game[cite: 1]. You pick a jersey, you scream at the screen, and you send fifteen bucks to some political consultant in Washington who has never set foot in Arkansas[cite: 1, 2]. Meanwhile, local infrastructure rots, working people get squeezed to death, and corporate PACs write the checks that keep everybody quiet[cite: 1, 2].

I'm done with that[cite: 1, 2].

The Stoic Opposition is a simple, decentralized project[cite: 2]. We aren't starting an underfunded third party that disappears after November[cite: 2]. We aren't telling people to go yell at primary debates[cite: 2]. We are going to find the empty rooms where party decisions are legally made, walk inside, take the seats, and pass rules that kick corporate money out of the building[cite: 2].

Core Principles

1. Go where you actually belong.

I don't believe in being a political chameleon. If you lean left, you need to be working inside the Democratic Party. If you lean right, you need to be working inside the Republican Party.

Even if we kicked every dime of corporate PAC cash out of politics tomorrow, we'd still have real disagreements about tax rates, zoning, and how big local government ought to be. That's fine. That's healthy. What isn't healthy is lying about what you believe just to win an election. We want honest people cleaning up their own house.

2. The blank slate is our biggest leverage.

Here's the secret the establishment doesn't want you to notice: nobody shows up to local party meetings.

In a red county, the local Democratic committee is often just four or five retirees in a library basement trying to keep the lights on. In a deep blue city, the local Republican committee is completely dead.

People look at that and see failure. I look at it and see a clean workbench. When a room is empty, you don't need a million dollars to win. You just need three to five serious people who show up, learn the rules, and take the open seats.

3. The non-negotiable rule: no corporate PAC cash.

We don't police people's personal beliefs, but we do have one iron rule[cite: 1, 2]. If you're organizing with us, you commit to passing a bylaw that bans corporate PAC money and registered lobbyist cash from your local party committee[cite: 2]. Period.

The Three Steps

  • Step 1: The Intake. We use an intake form to map where people live down to the county and precinct level. If three people in the same county sign up, they become an operational cell.
  • Step 2: The Audit and Filing. The cell calls the county clerk or the state party and asks for the official committee roster. They find the empty precinct seats. In most states, getting appointed or filing to run for an empty precinct seat takes five minutes and zero filing fees.
  • Step 3: The Floor Vote. Once seated, members introduce our model bylaw amendment to ban corporate PAC contributions[cite: 2]. We did it in Lonoke County, and it works[cite: 2]. Once the money is out, the grifters leave, and you can start running regular people for quorum court and city council[cite: 2].

You can't want things to stay like this[cite: 1]. It's time to show up.
