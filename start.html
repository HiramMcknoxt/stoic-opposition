Take a Seat

Run your own county audit.

You do not need permission, a title, or a following. You need five pieces of homework and one Tuesday night.

The audit, in five steps

  1. Find your committee. Every county has (or is supposed to have) a county party committee for each party. Find when and where yours meets, and who the chair and secretary are. If nobody can tell you, that is your first finding: the room is emptier than you thought.
  2. Get the roster and the bylaws. The roster tells you how many seats exist and how many are filled. The bylaws tell you how seats are claimed, what quorum is, and how rules get changed. Ask politely. Ask in writing if you have to.
  3. Count the chairs. Total seats, filled seats, vacant seats. Then do the quorum math with the calculator below. Most people discover the room is controlled by fewer people than attend a decent cookout.
  4. Attend once as an observer. Show up, sit down, take notes. Learn the procedure before you try to use it. The meeting-observation sheet on the Print page is built for exactly this visit.
  5. Claim a seat — and bring a quorum. File whatever the bylaws and state law require to fill a vacant seat for your precinct. Then recruit to the number the math gave you, and put the clean-money rule on the agenda under New Business.

The quorum calculator
