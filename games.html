The Stoic Opposition Presents

Systems You Can Play.

An educational video game suite.

Every game on this shelf makes one argument the only way an argument really lands: by handing you the controls. You do not play the hero. You play the operator — the candidate, the billing stack, the store manager, the line-drawer — and you discover, by trying to win, what the system pays for and what it punishes.

A post can be argued with. A system you have personally tried to beat cannot be un-experienced.

House rules for this shelf

Systems, not villains. No game on this shelf has an evil character. Every actor is responding to incentives you can see. The indictment is of the structure and its arithmetic.

No invented numbers. Every real figure in a game traces to a named source carried in that game’s credits, or is labelled, on its face, a scenario assumption you can inspect. If a number cannot be sourced, the game uses time, trust, or points instead.

The debrief is the argument. Each game ends with one screen: what you just did, in real life — the real case, the real mechanism, and a link to go deeper. Everything preachy lives there, or nowhere.

Free, and yours. Every game is a single file that runs in a browser, collects nothing, and phones nothing home. Copy them, fork them, put them on your own site. The point is the argument, not the traffic.
